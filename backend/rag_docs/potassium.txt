### 🔶 Potassium (K) Deficiency — Key Symptoms

- **Marginal leaf scorching or leaf edge burn** — edges turn yellow → brown → dry.
- Symptoms appear first on **older/lower leaves** (mobile nutrient).
- Yellowing starts at edges and tips, progressing inward.
- Leaf curling, weak stalks, and poor disease resistance.
- In fruit crops: poor size, low sweetness (TSS), weak color development.
- Lodging common in cereals (rice, wheat, maize) under severe deficiency.

📌 Difference from Magnesium Deficiency:
- **Potassium → leaf edge burn & scorching on old leaves**
- **Magnesium → interveinal yellowing, green veins on old leaves**

---

### 🌍 Soil & Environmental Causes

- Sandy/loamy soils with low clay content (low K retention).
- Heavy rainfall or excessive irrigation causes K leaching.
- High Calcium/Magnesium application competes with K uptake.
- Overuse of ammonium (NH₄⁺) fertilizers reduces K absorption.
- Continuous cropping without organic matter addition.

---

### 🌱 Soil Application (Long-Term Correction)

- Use **Muriate of Potash (MOP / KCl)** for most crops.
- Recommended basal dose:  
  **10–20 kg per acre (25–50 kg/ha)** depending on crop demand.
- For vegetable & fruit crops under deficiency:  
  **20–30 kg per acre (50–75 kg/ha)** split dose recommended.
- Mix MOP with **FYM/compost** and apply near root zone, not too close to stem.
- In chloride-sensitive crops (tobacco, grapes, potato), prefer **SOP (Sulfate of Potash)**.

---

### 💧 Foliar Spray (Quick Boost)

- Use **Potassium Nitrate (KNO₃) 1–2% foliar spray**.
- Preparation:  
  **10–20 g KNO₃ per litre of water** + mild wetting agent.
- Spray **evening or cloudy weather** for better absorption.
- Repeat **2–3 sprays at 10–12 day intervals** during fruit development.
- In banana & grapes, KNO₃ spray improves fruit size, color & sweetness.

---

### ⚠️ Precautions & Compatibility

- Avoid **excess MOP (KCl)** in chloride-sensitive crops (tobacco, grapes, potato).
- Do **not mix MOP** directly with **urea + DAP** during basal application.
- Do not spray KNO₃ in **very hot sunny hours** to avoid leaf burn.
- Balanced nitrogen is important — too much N reduces K uptake.
- Maintain adequate Magnesium; excess Potassium can cause **Mg deficiency**.

---

### 🍌 Crop-Specific Notes (Banana, Paddy, Grapes, Sugarcane)

- **Banana:** Marginal scorching & poor fruit filling → KNO₃ 1–2% spray during early bunch development; soil MOP split dose strongly recommended.
- **Paddy/Rice:** Weak stems and lodging at flowering stage → apply MOP with FYM; avoid applying during standing water without draining.
- **Grapes:** Chloride sensitivity → prefer **SOP** in soil; KNO₃ foliar spray improves berry sweetness.
- **Sugarcane:** Reduced cane girth & low juice quality → split application of MOP during tillering + grand growth stage.
