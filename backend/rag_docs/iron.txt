### ⚫ Iron (Fe) Deficiency — Key Symptoms

- Youngest leaves turn pale yellow between veins (interveinal chlorosis).
- Midrib stays dark green while leaf lamina turns yellow.
- Leaf tips remain green, but margins may become white in severe cases.
- Plant growth becomes weak and stunted due to reduced chlorophyll production.
- Symptoms appear first on the **youngest leaves**, unlike Magnesium or Nitrogen.

---

### 🌍 Soil & Environmental Causes

- Alkaline soils (pH > 7.5) or calcareous soils (lime-rich).
- Excess Phosphorus, Manganese, or Copper reduces iron availability.
- Waterlogged soils or poor drainage cause Fe oxidation (unavailable form).
- Sandy soils with low organic matter show more iron deficiency.
- High bicarbonate irrigation water reduces Fe uptake.

---

### 🌱 Soil Application (Long-Term Correction)

- Incorporate **Ferrous Sulphate (FeSO₄)** into the soil.
- Recommended dose: **25–50 kg per hectare (10–20 kg/acre)**.
- Mix FeSO₄ with **FYM/compost** for better nutrient chelation.
- Split application is beneficial in **high pH or calcareous soils**.
- For alkaline soils, consider **chelated Fe-EDTA or Fe-EDDHA** (more efficient).

---

### 💧 Foliar Spray (Quick Relief)

- Use **Ferrous Sulphate (FeSO₄) 0.5% + 0.25% Citric Acid** spray.
- Preparation per litre: **5 g FeSO₄ + 2.5 g citric acid**.
- Citric acid helps prevent chemical precipitation and improves absorption.
- Spray early morning/evening; avoid strong sunlight.
- Repeat **2–3 sprays at 10–12 day intervals** if symptoms continue.

---

### ⚠️ Precautions & Compatibility

- Do **not mix iron sprays** with **alkaline pesticides or Bordeaux mixture**.
- Avoid mixing ferrous sulphate with strong phosphate fertilizers during spraying.
- Water the soil before foliar application; never spray on dry stressed plants.
- Use chelated iron (Fe-EDTA/EDDHA) in **high pH soil** for better efficiency.

---

### 🍌 Crop-Specific Notes (Banana, Rice, Grapes, Groundnut)

- **Banana:** Yellowing of young leaves with green midrib → foliar FeSO₄ + citric acid spray gives fast recovery.
- **Rice:** Common in waterlogged fields → correct with FeSO₄ soil application + proper drainage.
- **Grapes:** Yellowing on young leaves in calcareous soils → prefer chelated iron spray for better uptake.
- **Groundnut:** Iron chlorosis leads to poor pegging → FeSO₄ soil application + organic matter addition recommended.
